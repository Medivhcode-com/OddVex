# Owner questions

prompt requires (`BUILD_PROMPT.md`, "Owner decisions"). Add a row when an item raises a new question; move a row to
"Answered" with the date and the Founder's words when it is settled.

OQ-07 to OQ-22 were collected while fixing the pre-launch audit findings, not from a backlog item. Where one needs a
lawyer as well as the Founder, the counsel packet (`docs/compliance/COUNSEL_REVIEW_PACKET.md`) asks the legal half.

| OQ-23 | Snapshot archive footprint and licence (P0.1, `docs/features/p0-1.md`). Confirm or change: (a) a capture every 6 minutes for odds and every hour for props, 7 days at full resolution, 400 days for each game's first, closing and last captures and for bet anchors, a cap of 1.5 GiB of counted data and a ceiling of 2 GiB on the files, on the shared 100 GB disk. Measured on PostgreSQL 16 with the synthetic schedule (20 games a day): 70.4 MB a day, so about 0.55 GB after the first week and about 0.95 GB by day 400; about 1.3 GB by day 400 if every game reaches the bet-anchor bound. A busier real schedule grows in proportion: at about 34 games a day (25 with every game at the anchor bound) the total reaches the cap; capture then stops and resumes once the prune has brought the archive back under it. The cap counts rows times measured bytes per row, not file size. On PostgreSQL the table files do not shrink after the prune (autovacuum makes the freed space reusable by later rows; only a `VACUUM FULL`, which blocks reads and writes of the tables while it runs, returns it to the disk, and nothing runs one), and until review round 4 the index files grew with every prune (270 MB of files for 18 MB of data after six test cycles). Now the capture job rebuilds an archive index once it is 1.3 times the size its rows need (`REINDEX INDEX CONCURRENTLY`, after the hourly prune, one index at a time; reads and writes go on), so the files stay within 1.25 times the largest counted size: at most 1.9 GiB with the counted size at the cap, and capture also stops at 2 GiB of files. Measured: 1.01 times over nine test cycles; in rolling operation 1.13 times with a VACUUM after every hourly prune, but a server with the default autovacuum settings vacuums a table only once a fifth of its rows are dead or new, and measured that way (ten synthetic days) the files reached 1.215 times the largest counted size (review round 4 saw 1.22 and 1.24 with its own script, review round 5 1.216 and 1.215 on two other schedules). So with the counted size at the cap expect the files at about 1.82 to 1.86 GiB, not the 1.7 GiB the 1.13 figure would suggest, and at most 1.9 GiB if the stated factor holds: the 2 GiB ceiling is the stop that holds, and the margin under it is thin. (The ratio to the counted size of the moment, which falls after every prune while the table files stay, reached 1.272 here and 1.308 in round 5; it is not a bound and an earlier text was wrong to multiply the cap by it.) Rolling operation was measured with retention shortened to 1 and 3 days, not at 7 and 400. To decide: keep the 2 GiB ceiling, raise it, or have the autovacuum thresholds of the three large tables lowered and measured (not done). The cost to confirm: a rebuild uses extra disk while it runs, one new copy of the index, the sort's temporary files and as much write-ahead log; for the largest index with the archive at its cap that was 256 MB, 265 MB and 232 MB for 14.5 s on the development machine, with the table in memory (review round 5 measured 225 MB of temporary files and 233 to 253 MB of log for the same rebuild). The preview's database has 512 MB for a table of about 1 GiB at the cap, so a rebuild there reads and writes the disk: review round 5 estimated 35 to 70 s, against a limit of 120 s; nobody has timed it there. A rebuild that runs into the limit fails (error class `QueryCanceled` in the administrators' statistics), is tried again after 6 hours, and writes and discards about 0.5 GiB each time. The limit (`ODDVEX_SNAPSHOT_RECLAIM_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`) and the ceiling (`ODDVEX_SNAPSHOT_MAX_DISK_BYTES`) are not among the keys `ACTION=setenv` can set: changing either on the preview takes a commit to `infrastructure/hostinger/compose.yaml` and a deploy, and an archive setting the application refuses stops the whole API from starting, flag on or off (the deploy then puts the previous release back); in the rolling tests, with retention shortened to one day, about three passes a day rebuilt an index (32 in ten days). A dump in progress makes rebuilds fail until it ends and can leave one extra copy of an index (256 MB at the cap) meanwhile; the next pass after it drops the copy. A rebuild delays that worker turn's alert evaluation and pick grading by its length. Production runs PostgreSQL 17; the mechanism was tested on 16 and checked against the 17 manual, which describes it in the same words; a CI job now runs the archive tests on 17 (first run: on the pull request). Nothing can be run by hand on the preview (nobody has a shell there and no ops action runs a script), and a forced check would rebuild nothing on a new archive anyway. The first rebuild on PostgreSQL 17 happens by itself after the first prune that deletes rows, a little over 7 days after the flag is turned on: someone should then read the `reclaim` entry of `GET /v1/admin/archive/stats` (`status` ok, `indexes_rebuilt` above 0, `finished_at` less `captured_at` as the time it took; a failure names the index and the error class). To decide: whether that is enough, or whether an ops action that rebuilds one index on demand should be built before the flag is turned on. (a2) The bet-anchor budget: at most 6 captures per game are kept for stored bets beyond 7 days, ranked by weight (each user with a bet on the game is one vote, split over the captures that user's bets point at); a bet whose capture was not kept answers "cannot price: archive pruned" after 7 days. Raise it (each capture per game costs up to 7.3 kB, about 0.06 GB over 400 days on the synthetic schedule), lower it, or keep it. (b) The archive is inside every database dump: one synthetic day compressed to 4.5 MB with `pg_dump -Fc`, and the kept rows of 700 pruned games to 2.29 MB (review round 4), so about 60 MB per dump by day 400 and about 1.15 GB across the 19 daily and pre-deploy dumps, nearer 100 MB a dump with the counted size at the cap; real prices may compress less well. Each restore also keeps the replaced database in the server (`oddvex_replaced_<time>`, up to about 1.9 GiB at the cap), and no ops action removes it: to decide, whether to add one. A dump holds rows, not free space, so it follows the archive's counted size; these figures assume it stays near the measured size, and a busier schedule scales them. Keep it in the dumps, or leave it out on the preview (on synthetic data it can be generated again; on a licensed feed it could not). (c) Live bets will mostly show "cannot price: stale at placement", because live quotes go stale after 120 s and captures are 6 minutes apart, until a live vendor exists (OQ-17). (d) Bets placed before the flag is turned on, or before capture covered their game, show "not captured"; there is no backfill. (e) For counsel, before a real provider is connected (with OQ-02 and OQ-03): whether the licence allows storing the vendor's prices and lineup or injury status for 7 and 400 days and showing them back to users as of a past time; archival and display rights are often separate terms. Turning the flag off also stops the prune and the index rebuilds, and so does a rollback to an image from before P0.1, so stored rows outlive the 7- and 400-day retention until a current image runs with the flag on again (or migration 0008 is downgraded, which drops the tables). By design a game's kept rows leave together, when its last capture is 400 days old, so the oldest of them is older than 400 days by the time the game was captured for: usually the 36 hours before its start and the game itself, but with no fixed upper bound for a game whose start keeps being moved (it stays in scope) or that is listed again later (the kept rows of its first stay wait for its last).
